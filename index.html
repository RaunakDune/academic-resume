About

I am a Professor of Practice in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Houston and a Geospatial Data Scientist with a decade-long focus on ML/AI. I particularly specialize in remote sensing, geotechnical engineering, geostatistics, agent-based modeling and visual place recognition. I have worked in blockchain-based edge computing, post-disaster recovery systems and Lunar exploration methodologies. My current research centres on visual navigation for rovers on the Moon. I enjoy lecturing and teaching undergraduate students.

Current Research

Galatea: Visual Place Recognition for the Lunar Surface

A two-stage system that lets a rover localize itself from camera images where no GNSS is available. Global retrieval aggregates frozen or fine-tuned DINOv2 features with a semi-relaxed optimal-transport head that generalizes NetVLAD and SALAD; candidates are then re-ranked with training-free fused Gromov–Wasserstein matching of keypoints. Evaluated on LunarSim-VPR, a new physically motivated benchmark rendering identical poses under seven Sun conditions, Galatea reaches 78.8% Recall@1 against 27–36% for zero-shot terrestrial VPR models. With Christoph F. Eick; manuscript in preparation.

  • Project website: LunarSim
  • Code & benchmark

Proteus: Post-Disaster Road Maps from Crowdsensed GPS Traces

Reference implementation of the Proteus system (GeoInformatica, 2026). After a disaster, volunteers’ GPS traces are cross-verified against one another and the pre-disaster road network to produce a map of passable roads. The open-source release extends the paper to real GPS traces and includes an agent-based simulator, a command-line interface and scripts that reproduce the paper’s experiments.
